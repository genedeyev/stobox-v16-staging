Glossary

78 words this industry uses at you, in plain English.

Every word here appears somewhere on this site, and each definition is written without using another piece of jargon to explain it. If one of these gets used at you in a meeting, this is what it means.

Core ideas

Asset Passport

The asset’s own file: every claim about it, the document behind each claim, and who signed that document. A buyer reads it instead of asking you for a pack, and it travels with the token.

Where it matters: Compass →

Asset tokenization

Issuing a blockchain token that represents legal ownership of, or a claim on, a real asset. The token becomes the official, transferable record of ownership.

Where it matters: What tokenization is →

Attestation

A dated statement of fact signed by a named outside party, such as a vault or a laboratory, attached to the record so a buyer can read it without asking you.

Where it matters: Mining and commodities →

Cap table

The list of everyone who owns a piece of the company, and how much. It is the asset itself in a private company, which is why it being wrong is expensive.

Where it matters: Technology →

Digital Security Offering (DSO)

Another name for an STO – an offering of digitally native, tokenized securities. The terms are used interchangeably; DSO emphasizes that the security's official record lives on-chain.

Where it matters: Raisable →

Master-feeder structure

A fund pattern for mixed investor bases: a US feeder for US taxable investors and an offshore feeder for everyone else, both investing into one master vehicle. Tokenization digitizes the feeders' registers without changing the tax logic.

Where it matters: Guides →

Non-custodial

A model where the user holds their own keys and assets. Stobox is non-custodial – it never takes custody of investor funds or tokens.

Where it matters: Compass →

On-chain registry

The blockchain-based cap table that records who owns a tokenized security in real time, replacing spreadsheets and manual transfer agents.

Where it matters: Compass →

Primary issuance

The first sale of a tokenized security by its issuer to investors – the capital-raising event, as opposed to later secondary trading.

Where it matters: Raisable →

Real-world asset (RWA)

A tangible or traditional financial asset – real estate, a fund, private equity, commodities, royalties – that exists off-chain and can be represented on a blockchain.

$27.9B on chain across 158 products, DefiLlama, 7 September 2026

Where it matters: What tokenization is →

Register

The official list of who owns what. Today it is a spreadsheet somebody maintains. After tokenization it is the asset itself, and it updates when ownership moves.

Where it matters: Real estate →

Secondary trading

Buying and selling after issuance. It happens through licensed venues, where the venue decides whether to list. A token makes a transfer possible inside the rules; it does not create a market.

Where it matters: Compass →

Security token

A token that is a regulated security – it confers ownership, profit, or repayment rights and is subject to securities law. Distinct from a utility token.

Where it matters: Compass →

Security Token Offering (STO)

A regulated sale of tokenized securities to investors under a securities exemption (e.g. Reg D, Reg S, Reg CF, Reg A+). Unlike a 2017-era ICO, an STO sells real, compliance-gated securities – not a utility coupon.

Where it matters: Raisable →

Self-custody wallet

A wallet the owner controls directly. On Stobox it signs you in, anchors one-time verification, and holds the securities you buy.

Where it matters: Compass →

Special purpose vehicle (SPV)

A company created to hold one asset (or one deal) and nothing else. In tokenization it is the standard wrapper: the SPV holds the asset's title, the tokens represent interests in the SPV – ring-fenced from the sponsor's creditors.

Where it matters: Guides →

Tokenized security

A security (equity, debt, fund interest) issued and recorded as a compliant on-chain token, with transfer rules enforced by the token itself.

Where it matters: Compass →

Utility token

A token used to access a product or service, conferring no equity, profit-share, or voting rights. A consumer good, not a security.

19,647 coins in existence, most of them this, CoinGecko, 7 September 2026

Where it matters: STBU →

Rules and who may invest

Accredited investor

An investor meeting income or net-worth thresholds who is permitted to participate in certain private (e.g. Reg D) offerings.

Where it matters: Compass →

Beneficial owner

The actual human being who ends up with the money. Rules on sanctions and money laundering apply to that person, not to the company in front of them.

Where it matters: Mining and commodities →

Broker-dealer

A licensed firm that transacts securities. Public raises are run through broker-dealers; Stobox is a technology provider, not a broker-dealer.

Where it matters: Raisable →

EU Prospectus Regulation

The EU framework governing when a published prospectus is required to offer securities to the public across member states.

Where it matters: Raisable →

Exemption

The rule that lets you sell shares to investors without registering a public offering. Your lawyer picks it. It decides who may invest and how you may advertise.

Where it matters: Real estate →

KYC / KYB

Know Your Customer / Know Your Business – the identity verification of investors and entities required before participating in a regulated offering.

Where it matters: Compass →

Lock-up

A period during which a holder has agreed not to sell. Also usually a document, and also enforceable by the asset itself.

Where it matters: Technology →

Reg A+

A U.S. exemption (a 'mini-IPO') allowing public raises up to a set ceiling from all investors, with SEC qualification and ongoing reporting.

Where it matters: Raisable →

Reg CF

U.S. Regulation Crowdfunding – lets companies raise from the general public (accredited and non-accredited) up to an annual cap, via a registered portal.

Where it matters: Raisable →

Reg D (Rule 506(c))

A U.S. exemption for private placements to accredited investors, allowing general solicitation when all investors are verified as accredited.

Where it matters: Raisable →

Reg S

A U.S. exemption for securities offered to investors outside the United States, commonly paired with Reg D for global raises.

Where it matters: Raisable →

Transfer agent

The party that maintains a security's ownership records. For tokenized securities, an on-chain registry performs this function automatically.

Where it matters: Compass →

Transfer restriction

A rule about whether an investor may sell their stake and to whom. It is in the fund’s own documents, and it is the first thing to read before promising anyone liquidity.

Where it matters: Investment funds →

Deals and documents

Appraisal

A valuation signed by a licensed valuer. Yours and theirs are different documents, and a buyer only counts the second.

Where it matters: Real estate →

Assay

A laboratory test of how much metal is actually there and how pure it is. An in-house test is an opinion; a licensed laboratory has a licence to lose.

Where it matters: Mining and commodities →

Carbon credit

A tradable certificate for emissions avoided or removed. It is a separate asset from the power, with separate rules and a separate buyer.

Where it matters: Renewable energy →

Change-of-control clause

A line in the loan that lets the bank object if the ownership of the borrower changes. It is why the lender is the first call, not the last.

Where it matters: Real estate →

Compute

The machines that run the models: graphics cards, servers and the building and power they need. Unlike a model, it is a physical thing you can own and finance.

Where it matters: Artificial intelligence →

Custody, or vault statement

A signed statement from the vault confirming what it is holding for you. Not the same document as one you write yourself, and buyers know the difference.

Where it matters: Mining and commodities →

Grid connection

Permission and physical capacity to put power into the network. Without it the plant is equipment in a field.

Where it matters: Renewable energy →

Model weights

The trained output of the model itself. They are hard to own cleanly, hard to transfer and hard to value on any given day, which is why we do not build offerings on them.

Where it matters: Artificial intelligence →

Offtake agreement

A contract with a buyer who agrees in advance to take what you produce. It turns a mine into a predictable stream of money.

Where it matters: Mining and commodities →

Offtaker

The party on the other side of that contract. Their credit quality, not yours, is what an investor is really taking a view on.

Where it matters: Renewable energy →

Option ledger

The record of shares promised to employees, on what terms and when they earn them. It sits outside the share register and is regularly forgotten in one of the two.

Where it matters: Technology →

Power purchase agreement

A long contract with someone who agrees to buy the electricity. It is the reason a solar field is financeable and a solar field without one is not.

Where it matters: Renewable energy →

Rent roll

The list of who rents what, for how much, and until when. It is the document that turns a building into an income figure.

Where it matters: Real estate →

Right of first refusal

A rule that says existing holders get the chance to buy before an outsider can. It is normally in a document somebody has to remember; written into the asset, it is enforced automatically.

Where it matters: Technology →

SAFE, and convertible note

Two ways of taking money now and deciding later how much of the company it bought. They are the most common reason a cap table and the paperwork disagree.

Where it matters: Technology →

The company that holds the building

Property is usually owned by a company, not by a person. You sell shares in that company, not bricks, and its own documents decide whether that is allowed.

Where it matters: Real estate →

Money in, money out

Audit right

The right of an investor to have somebody check the numbers behind their payment. Without it, the share of revenue is a promise rather than a claim.

Where it matters: Revenue and royalties →

Contracted revenue

Money customers have already signed up to pay you over a period, as opposed to money you expect. Lenders and investors treat the two completely differently.

Where it matters: Artificial intelligence →

Depreciation

How quickly the hardware loses its value. On compute it is fast, and any honest financing structure has to say what happens when it does.

Where it matters: Artificial intelligence →

Gross and net

Before costs, and after them. Almost every dispute about a revenue share is a dispute about which of the two was meant.

Where it matters: Revenue and royalties →

Partial exit

Selling some of your stake and keeping the rest, usually so a founder can take money off the table without losing the company.

Where it matters: Private equity →

Pledged, or encumbered

Already promised to somebody else, usually a lender, as security for a loan. Income that is pledged cannot be sold twice, and it is the surprise that kills deals in week six.

Where it matters: Revenue and royalties →

Revenue share

The same idea stated as a percentage of money coming in, before costs. What counts as revenue has to be defined precisely, or it becomes the argument.

Where it matters: Revenue and royalties →

Royalty

A right to a share of what something earns, without owning the thing itself. Music, patents, software and minerals are the common ones.

Where it matters: Revenue and royalties →

Waterfall

The agreed order in which money is paid out: who is paid first, who is paid from what is left, and who is paid last.

Where it matters: Revenue and royalties →

Funds and vehicles

Carry

The share of profits a manager keeps. We never take it, on any deal, at any layer. It is a manager’s economics, not a technology provider’s.

Where it matters: Private equity →

Continuation vehicle

A new fund set up to buy assets from an older one, so the manager can hold them longer. Investors who want out are paid; investors who want to stay roll in.

Where it matters: Private equity →

Feeder fund

A second fund that exists only to pool a group of investors and put them into the main one, usually because they sit in a different country or a different rule set.

Where it matters: Investment funds →

Limited partner

An investor in a fund. They put in money and take no part in running it, which is what limits their liability.

Where it matters: Investment funds →

Portfolio company

One of the businesses a fund or a holding company owns. The value is in these, not in the vehicle that holds them.

Where it matters: Private equity →

Side letter

A private agreement giving one investor terms the others do not have. Most funds have several, and together they quietly override parts of the main agreement.

Where it matters: Investment funds →

Subscription documents

The pack an investor signs to join a fund: who they are, what they are allowed to invest in, and what they are agreeing to.

Where it matters: Investment funds →

The mark

What the manager says the portfolio is currently worth. It is an opinion until somebody independent signs one, which is why an outside valuation changes the conversation.

Where it matters: Private equity →

Standards, chains and rails

Arbitrum

An Ethereum Layer-2 network. The STBX security token is issued on Arbitrum.

Where it matters: STBX →

Base

The USDC-native Ethereum Layer-2 in the Coinbase stack. Stobox Compass issues on Base for compliant, low-cost, scalable settlement.

Where it matters: Compass →

ERC-20

The baseline Ethereum standard for fungible tokens. Compliant securities extend it with permissioning and transfer restrictions.

Where it matters: What tokenization is →

Permissioned token standards

Token standards that carry identity and eligibility rules, so only a holder the rules admit can receive one. ERC-3643 and ERC-7943 are the two that matter; Stobox is a backer of ERC-7943.

Where it matters: What tokenization is →

Smart wallet

A self-custody wallet (e.g. Coinbase Smart Wallet) with no seed phrase to record, used to sign in and hold assets on Stobox.

Where it matters: Compass →

USDC

A fully-reserved, dollar-backed stablecoin used to settle investments in tokenized assets on Stobox.

$74.7B in circulation, DefiLlama, 7 September 2026

Words we do not use

The other half of a house style, which nobody usually prints.

A glossary says what words mean. This says which ones are ruled out here, and what we say instead. Each is a decision somebody can hold us to.

  1. Seamless, frictionless, effortless

    Nothing here is any of those. The work is paperwork and it takes as long as it takes.

  2. Cutting-edge, next-generation, world-class

    Adjectives doing a number’s job. We publish the number instead, or say we do not have one.

  3. Bank-grade security

    Not a standard, not an audit, not a certification. It means nothing, so it is not on this site.

  4. Unlock liquidity

    A token does not create a buyer. We say a path to secondary trading through licensed venues, and that listing rests with the venue.

  5. Make your assets smart

    The asset stays what it was. The rules around it are what become enforceable.

  6. Put your data on the blockchain

    The asset and its rule set go on chain. The canonical record stays off it, and that is the part most descriptions skip.

  7. Our product Raisable

    Raisable belongs to Raisable Inc. The only wording we use is built with Raisable, distributed by Stobox.

  8. A percentage of your raise

    Taking a share of what you raise is broker-dealer work, and it carries its own regulatory weight. Our fees are flat and published.

These are enforced by a script, not by taste. A build that reintroduces one of them fails before it ships.
