What are Digital Assets?

Digital assets, images, and videos are gaining importance as valuable, identifiable property subject to rights. Discover their impact.

Stobox Research

5 July 2022 · 9 min read

Digital assets are anything created and existing in digital form that can be identified, detected, and has value. They are rapidly gaining in popularity and importance. Information, images, videos, and more are considered digital assets subject to property rights.

In this article, you will learn about digital assets, their importance, how they affect our lives, the concept of ownership, and human interaction.

Definition and Types of Digital Assets

A digital asset is an object that exists in a digital form and has some value, at least for one person. Digital assets are a much broader concept today than images, documents, or audio files. Blockchain technology has changed everything. The blockchain itself is a distributed ledger protected by a consensus algorithm. This concept was implemented before but has become a more advanced solution than previous options. Distributed ledgers have been around for a while and are used by different companies. However, the blockchain has made this technology available to ordinary users.

For a digital asset to be considered an asset, it must meet the critical requirement of having the potential to create value, at least to the owner. It is also important that the asset can be transferred to someone. For example, you can sell, donate, exchange assets for something, etc.

Types of Digital Assets

Today, digital assets include objects created or stored on the blockchain. These include cryptocurrencies and tokens. There are also so-called non-fungible tokens (NFTs), generally used for digital art objects. 

Cryptocurrencies

Cryptocurrencies are decentralized digital currencies that use cryptographic methods to secure transactions and control the creation of new units. Examples include Bitcoin (BTC), Ethereum (ETH), and Litecoin (LTC). Cryptocurrencies are used as a medium of exchange, store of value, and unit of account in decentralized networks, allowing peer-to-peer transactions without intermediaries such as banks.

Non-fungible tokens (NFTs)

NFTs are unique digital assets representing the ownership or proof of authenticity of a particular item or piece of content, usually stored on a blockchain. Unlike cryptocurrencies, NFTs are not fungible, meaning each token is separate and cannot be exchanged for another. They are used to tokenize digital art, collectibles, virtual real estate, and other unique assets. Each NFT contains metadata that defines its uniqueness, making it valuable to collectors and creators.

Digital tokens

Digital tokens represent a wide range of assets or rights and may or may not be fungible. They are often issued and managed on blockchain platforms, which provide partial ownership, liquidity, and programmable features. 

Digital tokens can represent ownership of physical assets (e.g., real estate, commodities), financial instruments (e.g., stocks, bonds), or utility in a particular ecosystem (e.g., loyalty points, gaming assets). Examples include security tokens, utility tokens, asset-backed tokens, and stablecoins.

What is Digital Asset Tokenization?

Tokenization is converting the rights to an asset into a digital token on a blockchain. Simply put, it's like converting physical assets or rights (such as real estate, stocks, or even works of art) into digital tokens easily traded or transferred over the Internet. Each token represents a share of ownership or other form of right in the underlying asset.

Why is this important in the digital asset space? First, tokenization opens up access to assets that were previously illiquid or difficult to divide. For example, owning real estate traditionally requires significant capital and is often associated with complexities such as paperwork and legal processes. With tokenization, you can own a piece of property represented by tokens, making real estate investments more accessible to a wider range of investors.

Second, tokenization increases liquidity. Since these digital tokens can be traded on blockchain platforms, buying, selling, and transferring ownership of assets becomes much easier and faster. This liquidity aspect can attract more investors and potentially increase the value of investments.

Tokenization provides transparency and security of asset ownership. Blockchain technology ensures that ownership records are immutable and transparent, reducing the risk of fraud and providing a clear audit trail of transactions.

How Digital Assets are Tokenized

The technical process of digital asset tokenization includes several steps, which may differ depending on the type of asset being tokenized and the blockchain platform used.

  • Choosing a platform for tokenization. Ethereum is one of the most popular platforms for tokenization due to its support for smart contracts. Still, other platforms, such as Binance Smart Chain, Polkadot, or Solana, can be chosen based on specific requirements.
  • Depending on the asset and its requirements, developers choose a token standard. For example, Ethereum-based tokens typically conform to ERC-20 standards for fungible tokens (e.g., cryptocurrencies) or ERC-721 for non-fungible tokens (e.g., digital collectibles).
  • Determining how tokens will represent the digital asset. For example, if you are tokenizing real estate, each token can represent a share of ownership or rights to it.
  • Depending on the jurisdiction and the nature of the asset, developers should ensure compliance with relevant regulations. This can include implementing KYC (Know Your Customer) and AML (Anti-Money Laundering) in smart token contracts.
  • Once smart contracts are designed, and compliance measures are implemented, tokens are issued to represent ownership or rights to the digital asset. This issuance process usually involves minting a certain number of tokens and assigning them specific addresses.
  • Once issued, tokens can be distributed to investors through token sales, token drops, or other means. Once in circulation, tokens can be traded on cryptocurrency exchanges or decentralized exchanges (DEXs), providing liquidity for investors.
  • Tokenized assets may require ongoing management and control. Smart contracts can be programmed to pay dividends, voting rights, or other functions related to asset ownership.

Digital Assets in Use

Digital assets today form an important part of modern transactions, driving innovation and changing how people interact with digital services and assets. Cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin and Ethereum are being accepted by many traders worldwide. This allows users to make payments for everything from everyday products to luxury goods and travel services. 

Digital tokens make accessing various digital services and platforms easier, such as decentralized finance (DeFi) protocols, gaming ecosystems, and content creation platforms. Non-fungible tokens (NFTs) are reshaping the ownership and trading of digital artwork, collectibles, and virtual assets, providing creators with new sources of income and collectors with a unique ownership experience. Using stablecoins ensures price stability in transactions and enables direct transfers across borders, promoting financial inclusion and reducing the cost of transfers.

Digital Assets in the Investment Sphere

Unlike traditional assets, digital assets function in decentralized networks, which opens up new opportunities for investors to diversify their portfolios.

Digital assets have stimulated the development of new investment strategies and financial products, such as crypto-index funds, futures contracts, and exchange-traded funds (ETFs). These instruments fill the gap between traditional finance and the digital asset ecosystem, offering investors access to the market while mitigating some risks.

Legal and Regulatory Aspects

The legal aspect of digital assets is a dynamic and complex space characterized by challenges and progress. Regulators worldwide are challenged with developing a regulatory structure that considers the unique characteristics of digital assets while protecting investors and maintaining market integrity.

One important problem is the lack of uniformity in regulation across jurisdictions. The general absence of agreed standards makes compliance difficult for businesses operating in the digital asset space, leading to regulatory fragmentation and confusion.

Another barrier is the classification of digital assets within existing regulatory frameworks. Regulators must determine whether these assets should be categorized as securities, commodities, currency, or a new asset class. This classification shapes the regulatory rules and obligations imposed on market participants.

Ensuring investor protection is paramount in the digital asset ecosystem. Its inherent risks, such as price volatility, fraud, and cybersecurity threats, emphasize the need for robust investor safeguards.

Regulators must maintain market integrity. Combating market manipulation, insider trading, and fraud in digital asset markets requires sophisticated supervisory, enforcement, and collaboration with industry stakeholders.

Despite these challenges, significant progress has been made in regulating digital assets. Some jurisdictions have introduced comprehensive regulatory frameworks tailored to digital assets, providing clarity for market participants and facilitating industry growth. Regulators are increasingly engaging with industry stakeholders to gather information and co-create regulations, reflecting a more proactive and inclusive approach to regulation.

Compliance for Digital Assets

Compliance requirements cover a range of regulations to protect investors, prevent financial crime, and ensure market integrity. Here's how compliance requirements affect the use and trading of digital assets:

  • Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Know Your Customer (KYC). AML and KYC regulations require digital asset service providers, such as exchanges and wallet providers, to implement robust procedures to verify the identity of their customers and monitor transactions for suspicious activity. 
  • Licensing and registration. Licensing requirements vary depending on the nature of the business activity, with exchanges, custodians, and other service providers typically subject to different regulatory regimes. Compliance with licensing requirements ensures that the business operates within the law.
  • Securities regulation. Securities legislation regulates aspects such as disclosure of information on securities offerings, trading venues, and investor protection to ensure fair and transparent markets.
  • Compliance with tax laws. The taxation of digital assets varies from jurisdiction to jurisdiction, with tax authorities imposing reporting requirements and tax liabilities on digital asset transactions.

The list of blockchain-based digital assets is quite extensive. These assets already play an essential role in our lives. Unfortunately, some problems prevent a significant leap in the development of blockchain and cryptocurrencies. Still, even despite the lack of a common legislative framework, these technologies continue to develop and improve gradually.
