The part nobody else does

Every answer says who is behind it, not how good it sounds.

This is the difference between a data room and a record. A number in a spreadsheet and the same number in an audited statement are not the same fact, and pretending otherwise is exactly what collapses in diligence.

  • T0Unverified

    Your word

    You told us. Nothing behind it yet.

  • T1Unverified

    Your summary

    A deck, a memo, a sheet you prepared.

  • T2Traceable

    Your systems

    Pulled from software you run, not retyped by hand.

  • T3Traceable

    Your books

    The accounting system of record.

  • T4Proven

    Signed by the other side

    A contract, a confirmation, a countersignature.

  • T5Proven

    Issued by an authority

    A registry, a regulator, a licensed valuer, an auditor.

A tier says who stands behind an answer, never how good it is. Nothing here is marked as an opinion, and nothing is a judgement about you. The practical effect: the conversation stops being an argument about whether a number is right, and becomes a short list of what to go and get.

What it looks like when it is built

A record is a graph, whether or not anyone draws it.

Below is the record of one company, drawn live. Twelve divisions, their units and teams, and under those the things a business actually keeps, down to a single line on an invoice. Blue is what the company owns and signs. Navy is the world it has to watch: regulators, the market, suppliers, everything arriving from outside. Drag it, zoom into it, open any page.

Nothing here is a picture of a product. It is the shape of the thing itself: every page linked to the ones it belongs with, down to a single line on an invoice. A folder tree holds the same documents and can show you none of these connections.

The obvious objection

Why not just do this yourself with AI?

You can, and for some work you should. We are not competing with the model, we are built on one. Four things a chat window does not give you.

  • The list of questions

    Which facts capital actually requires, at which level of proof, in which order. That came out of eight years of deals and several hundred recorded decisions, and a prompt does not reconstruct it.

  • Checking, not generating

    Generated is not verified. A verified answer means a process somebody is answerable for, and that is what the other side is actually trusting.

  • It has to hold still

    A conversation drifts and cannot be cited six months later. A record is versioned, dated, and can be walked back through.

  • The other side has to accept it

    Do it yourself and you have built one more bespoke data room, which is the thing you were trying to stop building.

The honest version: if this is a one-off and no capital event is coming, doing it yourself is enough, and we will tell you so on the call rather than sell you something.

Who does the work

Our specialists build it with you, and it becomes one canonical record of your company.

Not a tool you are handed and left with. People who have done this on more than a hundred companies build one canonical layer of what is true about yours, with the evidence attached, and then keep it standing. Everything after that reads from it: a raise, a lender, an audit, a sale, the next asset.

  • 01

    They sit with your paperwork

    Someone reads what you actually have: the articles, the amendments nobody filed, the two valuations that disagree. You are not left to interpret a checklist on your own.

  • 02

    They decide what counts as proof

    Which document answers which question, and at which tier. That judgement is the product. It came out of eight years of deals, and it is the reason the other side accepts the record.

  • 03

    They keep it alive

    A record that is right in March and stale in September is worth nothing in the room. The same people carry it forward as the company changes, so it is current the day you need it.

One canonical layer, one place the answer comes from. Ask it what you own, what it is worth, and who has a claim on it, and the answer arrives with the document behind it rather than with somebody's recollection.
